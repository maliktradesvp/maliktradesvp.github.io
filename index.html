How it works

  • Everything runs at your live win rate: your win rate minus the discrepancy. The tool tries every whole number of NQ contracts your drawdown can take, plus step-up plans that add one contract once you're a third or two-thirds of the way to target. It plays each one out over thousands of evaluations.
  • Sessions aren't all equal: each session's win rate swings up to 10 points either side of your live rate, while the average stays the same. That's what makes a loss cap useful. On a bad session it stops you before the losses pile up.
  • Loss cap advice compares stopping after 1, 2 or 3 losses against no cap, on the same simulated sessions. Yes means a cap doesn't lower your pass chance and fits the plan's time budget: up to 50% extra time for Conservative, 25% for Balanced, 10% for Fast. The tightest cap that fits is recommended, because it gives the most protection on bad days. Optional means a cap only helps by costing more time than that. No means every cap lowers your odds.
  • Conservative is the quickest size within 3 points of the best possible pass chance. Fast is the quickest within 30 points. Balanced sits between them.
  • Each trade risks a stop between 30 and 40 ticks and wins your R:R times that, less about $4.50 commission per NQ contract. The drawdown is intraday trailing: it follows your highest balance including open profit. Losing trades are assumed to run up to halfway to target before reversing to the stop, which drags the floor up the way it does live. The eval passes the moment you reach the target.
  • Trading days count sessions you actually trade. Times are for the accounts that pass; accounts that drift sideways tend to bust instead, which is why passes run a bit quicker than target ÷ average daily profit.
  • The simulation treats trades as independent. If your losses come in streaks, real odds are lower than shown and a loss cap is worth more than the numbers say.
Educational tool, not financial advice. Prop firm rules change often; confirm your target and drawdown with your firm before you buy an evaluation.
